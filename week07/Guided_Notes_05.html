Guided Notes 5: Inferential Statistical Tests

NotePurpose

Use this guide with the Week 7-9 lectures and R demonstrations. It connects a business question and its variables to the appropriate inferential test, eco230r function, null hypothesis, and evidence needed for interpretation.

Keep the completed guide as a reference for homework, project analysis, quizzes, and the practicum.

Objectives

  • match analytical stories and variable types to inferential tests;
  • write an appropriate eco230r function call;
  • state null and alternative hypotheses in context;
  • interpret p-values, effect sizes, Bayes factors, and confidence intervals;
  • use method-specific follow-up evidence appropriately; and
  • identify limitations that affect a practical conclusion.

1) Tests for differences and benchmarks

Complete the final column as each method is introduced.

Analytical story Variables and design Test and eco230r function Null hypothesis Your decision rule or notes
Compare means for 2 independent groups 1 quantitative outcome; 1 categorical variable with 2 independent groups Independent-samples t-test: idt(y ~ group) \(H_0: \mu_A = \mu_B\)
Compare paired means 1 quantitative outcome measured twice or in matched pairs Paired-samples t-test: pst(~ before, ~ after) \(H_0: \mu_D = 0\)
Compare distributions or medians for 2 independent groups Quantitative or ordinal outcome; 2 independent groups; mean is inappropriate Wilcoxon rank-sum test: idw(y ~ group) \(H_0\): The population distributions are equal.
Compare paired distributions or median differences Quantitative or ordinal paired measurements Wilcoxon signed-rank test: psw(~ before, ~ after) \(H_0\): The population distribution of paired differences is centered at 0.
Compare means across more than 2 groups 1 quantitative outcome; 1 categorical variable with more than 2 groups One-way ANOVA: ano(y ~ group) \(H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \cdots = \mu_k\)
Compare one mean with a benchmark 1 quantitative variable and benchmark \(\mu_0\) One-sample t-test: ost(~ y, mu = mu_0) \(H_0: \mu = \mu_0\)

2) Tests for categorical distributions and relationships

Analytical story Variables and design Test and eco230r function Null hypothesis Your decision rule or notes
Rank or compare category frequencies against equal proportions 1 categorical variable Chi-square goodness-of-fit test: csf(~ category) \(H_0\): The population proportions are equal across categories.
Compare an observed distribution with specified benchmark proportions 1 categorical variable and expected proportions Chi-square goodness-of-fit test: csf(~ category, probs = c(...)) \(H_0\): The population distribution matches the expected proportions.
Examine association between categorical variables 2 categorical variables Chi-square test of independence: csi(outcome ~ group) \(H_0\): The variables are independent in the population.

When using probs = c(...), list expected proportions as decimals in the same order as the variable’s categories. The proportions must sum to 1.
