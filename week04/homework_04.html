Homework 4: Project Story Planning

NotePurpose

Turn descriptive statistics and visuals into a story someone can act on.

You will propose 5 candidate data stories. Each completed story is an analysis plan outline your team can use to select and combine ideas for the project.

Objectives

  • Write takeaway-first headlines for a business audience.
  • Identify relationships and the variables needed to investigate them.
  • Distinguish existing, derived, and external variables.
  • Draft testable hypotheses and feasible analyses.
  • Connect each story to a short explanation and a visual.

Resources

  • Open Homework 4 in Posit Cloud. Using the template is encouraged; Posit Cloud is optional. You may use Word or another document editor with the same headings.
  • Your project dataset: use the matching course .csv extract in Posit Cloud or the course dataset downloads.
  • Homework 2 tables, Homework 3 visuals, and Week 4 readings in Canvas.
  • Week 4 slides: Telling Stories with Data.
TipExpectations

This is planning. Do not run new tests for this homework. Bullets and concise notes are appropriate. Aim for clear, testable ideas and feasible analyses; identify any missing data that would be needed.

1) Confirm Your Project Dataset

In Dataset and filters, name your project dataset and list any quick filters you expect to apply, such as a date range or excluding blanks. Use short bullets.

2) Complete 5 Story Plans

Use the Story 1 through Story 5 sections in the Homework 4 template. Complete Parts 1-3 below for every story. These parts together are the analysis plan outline; you do not need a separate outline or a second copy of your headlines and relationships.

Part 1: Headline, relationships, and variables

Headline: Write one complete sentence stating a potential takeaway your dataset could support. Avoid numbers and test statistics. At this stage, the headline is a candidate claim to investigate.

  • Strong example: Late Olist deliveries receive lower review scores than on-time deliveries.
  • Weak example: Olist delivery and reviews.

The strong example gives a claim to evaluate; the weak example only names a topic.

Relationships 1-3: List 3 comparisons or patterns that could support or challenge the headline. For each relationship, list its variables and label each:

  • Exists: Already in the dataset.
  • Derived: You will calculate or recategorize it.
  • External: You would need another data source and a join.

For example, a Chicago crash story might examine injury proportion by lighting condition, injury proportion by road surface, and median injury count by time period. The first relationship uses reported_lighting_condition (Exists) and an any-injury indicator calculated from injuries_total (Derived).

Part 2: Short versions for your audience

  • 3-second version: Compress the takeaway to 7 words maximum, with no numbers.
  • 30-second version: Write 1-2 sentences giving the takeaway, who should care, and why.

For example: Crash injury patterns vary by lighting conditions.

Part 3: Hypotheses, data needs, and planned evidence

  • Hypotheses: Write at least 2 testable statements for the story. For example, the proportion of crashes involving injuries differs by lighting condition.
  • Data requirements and gaps: Use the variable list in Part 1. Explain how you would create derived fields, any external source and join key you would need, and likely issues such as missing values, outliers, or miscoded categories. Do not repeat the variable list.
  • Planned analyses: List methods that fit the relationships, such as group means or medians, proportions, time trends, or possible later tests (t-test, chi-square, ANOVA, or simple regression). You are choosing possible methods, not carrying out new tests.
  • Planned visual: Name one chart type and the comparison it would show. You are encouraged to reuse work from Homework 2 and Homework 3. Examples include a sorted bar chart of proportions, a time trend with consistent denominators, a boxplot, or a scatterplot.

Repeat all three parts for each of your 5 stories.

3) Prepare for Group Discussion

Rank your top 3 stories in Group prep. Be ready to share at least one with your team using its 3-second headline, 30-second explanation, and one visual idea. Your individual story plans will serve as a template for the team’s project analysis plan next week.

Submission

Submit one Word or PDF document in Canvas containing your dataset and filters, all 5 story plans, and your top-three ranking. If you use Posit Cloud, render and submit Homework_04_IP.pdf. The .qmd source file is not required. Keep your document available for the in-class group discussion.
