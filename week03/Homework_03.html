Homework 3: Visualizing Data

NotePurpose

This assignment helps you practice the Week 3 question:

How do we choose a visual that clearly communicates the comparison we want to make?

You will create three visuals, explain your analytical and design choices, and summarize what the visuals reveal. You will also reflect on responsible AI use that supports your work while keeping the analytical decisions yours.

Objectives

  • Choose chart types that match the analytical question.
  • Apply clear scales, category ordering, purposeful color, and readable labels.
  • State the data scope, units, filters, and percentage denominators.
  • Interpret evidence from your visuals in context.
  • Evaluate and disclose your use of AI.

Resources

  • Week 3 slides: visual perception and analytical frames
  • Week 1-2 slides on grain, measurement scales, and descriptive summaries
  • Your Week 1-2 dataset or another of the five approved course datasets
  • Course dataset descriptions and downloads
  • Lab 3 Hints and the Lab 3 video guide in Canvas
  • Optional R workspace: Homework 3 in Posit Cloud. Use this if you choose R or want a Quarto document template. Posit Cloud is optional; Homework 3 is required.
ImportantWhat to turn in

Submit one Word or PDF document with all three visuals embedded, each visual’s explanation, your summary, and your AI reflection. Also include the supporting files for the tools you used. Separate chart files or links do not replace the visuals inside your document.

1) Choose Your Dataset

Choose one of the five Week 1 datasets. You may keep the dataset from Homework 1 and 2 or switch. Briefly state the dataset, what one row represents, and any filters or cleaning you applied, such as excluding blanks or limiting dates.

Use the same dataset for all three visuals so your summary can connect them.

2) Choose Your Tools

Build the visuals using Excel, Tableau, R, or a combination. Choose the tool that supports the comparison you want to make; coding is not required.

Export readable chart images and insert them into Word or your document editor. If you use the optional Posit Cloud template, upload the images and insert them in the QMD, or render your R charts directly into the document.

3) Visual A - Comparison

Choose one analytical frame:

  • Nominal comparison: Compare values across categories, such as side-by-side bars.
  • Ranking: Order categories by a measure, such as sorted horizontal bars.

Label axes and units. Sort categories when rank is part of the question. Use a zero baseline for bars so their lengths represent the values honestly.

Include Visual A in your document. Directly below it, write 3-5 sentences addressing:

  1. Story: What comparison or pattern are you highlighting?
  2. Frame and encoding: Which analytical frame and chart type did you choose, and why do they fit?
  3. Data scope: Which groupings, filters, units, and denominators matter?
  4. Design: Name at least two design principles you applied and explain how they help the reader.

4) Visual B - Composition or Difference

Choose one analytical frame:

  • Part-to-whole: Use percentage bars with a clearly stated denominator and complete categories totaling 100%.
  • Time series: Show one metric over time, using chronological dates and a readable, honest scale.
  • Deviation: Show differences from a clearly labeled benchmark, with a zero line for the deviations.

Include Visual B in your document, followed by 3-5 sentences using the same structure: story, frame and encoding, data scope, and at least two design principles.

Hint: State what your percentages divide by. For a time series, keep month-year combinations distinct unless you intentionally compare seasonality. For deviation, explain why the benchmark is meaningful.

5) Visual C - Distribution or Relationship

Choose one analytical frame:

  • Distribution: Use a histogram with sensible bins or a box plot to show a numerical variable’s distribution.
  • Relationship: Use a scatterplot of two numerical variables; a trend line is optional when appropriate.

Include Visual C in your document, followed by 3-5 sentences using the same four-part structure. Label units, check what one mark represents, reduce overplotting when needed, and investigate unusual values before removing them. A relationship in the chart does not establish causation.

6) Review Your Design

Check all three visuals before assembling the final document:

  • The chart type matches the analytical frame.
  • Titles, axes, units, scales, and denominators are clear.
  • Category ordering and color support the comparison.
  • The chart avoids 3D effects and unnecessary decoration.
  • Bins, percentage calculations, benchmarks, and aggregation match the question.
  • Labels and values remain readable at the size used in the document.

This checklist is a review aid; you do not need to paste it under every chart.

7) Summary (Required)

In no more than 200 words, synthesize what your three visuals reveal. Reference Visuals A, B, and C, interpret the evidence, and explain whether the results match your expectations or show something unexpected.

Your summary belongs in the same document as the three embedded visuals and their explanations. Submit the complete document, not a summary by itself. The 200-word limit applies only to this summary, not the chart explanations or AI reflection.

8) Responsible Use of AI (Required Reflection)

AI may help with clarification, rewriting, or troubleshooting. It must not choose your analytical questions, make analytical decisions, or interpret results for you. You are responsible for checking every claim and submitting work you understand.

Write 4-6 sentences in your document:

  • State whether you used AI and name the tool if you did.
  • If you used it, describe a specific step it helped with, include one prompt you used, and explain what it missed, oversimplified, or got wrong. Describe how you checked or corrected its output.
  • If you did not use AI, say so, explain how you checked one chart or analytical choice yourself, and identify a task where AI might help and a decision you would keep your own. You do not need to invent a prompt or use AI to earn credit.

Submission

Upload to the Homework 3 assignment in Canvas:

  1. One Word (.docx) or PDF document containing your dataset/scope notes, Visuals A, B, and C embedded at a readable size, a 3-5 sentence explanation below each visual, the summary of no more than 200 words, and the 4-6 sentence AI reflection.
  2. Supporting files for the tools you used: Excel .xlsx; Tableau packaged workbook .twbx; or R/Quarto source (.R, .Rmd, or .qmd) with any image files needed to reproduce the document. Submit the applicable files, not files from tools you did not use.

If you use the optional Posit template, render Homework_03_IP.qmd to Homework_03_IP.pdf and submit that PDF as your complete document. Open the final Word or PDF file before submitting and confirm that all three visuals are visible without following links or opening a supporting workbook.
