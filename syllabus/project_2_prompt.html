Project 2: Research Proposal

Overview

Your team will choose a problem or opportunity and propose a research study that could help a decision maker address it. Explain what you would study, how you would collect information, and what the proposed design would allow you to conclude.

ImportantScope

This is a study-design and research-proposal exercise. You are not carrying out the research. Do not collect data, recruit participants, conduct a survey or experiment, or run analyses for this project. Describe what you would do if the proposed study were carried out.

Deliverables and Points

Deliverable When Points
Informal topic meeting Week 13, in class 10
Research proposal pitch Week 14, in class 10
Red Team critique exercise Week 14, in class 10
Written Research Proposal Before the final exam; see Canvas for your section’s deadline 70
Total 100

The Written Research Proposal is one document, about 2-4 pages in paragraph form. Use subheadings to organize the required components below. The proposal outline is a planning guide for that paper, not a separate submission. An outline or executive summary alone does not replace the paper.

Timeline

Week 12 - Choose Teammates and Explore Topics

Decide who you will work with and start discussing possible topics between this week and next. Identify a problem, opportunity, or decision that your team could develop into a research proposal.

Week 13 - Informal Topic Meeting

Bring a proposed topic to the in-class discussion. This meeting is worth 10 points. Your team should be ready to explain the problem, the research question, who or what you would study, a possible source of information, and why the proposed method fits the question.

Use the discussion to narrow the topic and work through design choices.

Week 14 - Pitch and Critique

Present your proposed study and participate in the critique exercise. Use the feedback and your responses to revise the written proposal.

Before the Final Exam - Written Research Proposal

Submit your complete proposal in the Project 2 Canvas assignment. Include all teammates’ names. The assignment uses a text-entry box, so paste the complete paper there with its subheadings. Check Canvas for your section’s deadline.

Choosing a Topic

You may use a business situation you know through work or personal experience, a question connected to your major, or another topic that interests your team. Identify who would use the results and what decision the study could inform.

You may propose a method beyond those practiced in class if you can explain how it works and why it fits the question. Keep the design specific enough that someone could understand how the study would be conducted.

In-Class Pitch and Critique

Teams will work in small sets of two or three teams. One team presents while the other team or teams critique the proposal. Rotate so every team presents and critiques. In a three-team set, two teams critique each presentation.

Research Proposal Pitch - 10 Points

Explain the problem, research question, proposed study design, reasons for your design choices, and the main risks or limitations. Respond to questions about how you would sample, measure, and collect information.

Red Team Critique Exercise - 10 Points

Examine the other team’s sampling, measures, design, conclusions, and ethical considerations. Identify specific weaknesses and suggest changes the team could make. Discuss your feedback with the presenting team.

The critique is part of the in-class exercise. Use the Canvas Red Team review activity during class. Its 10 points are included in the Project 2 rubric.

Written Research Proposal

Write one 2-4 page paper in paragraph form covering the following topics. These are sections of the same paper. They are not separate assignments or files. Describe the proposed work using language such as “we would sample” and “the study would measure.”

1. Problem or Opportunity

Describe the situation, why it matters, and the decision it could inform. Identify the decision maker or other people who would use the results.

2. Research Question

State a focused question that your study could answer. Explain its scope and how the answer would help with the decision. Short research objectives or a tentative hypothesis may help clarify your question.

3. Proposed Study Design and Measures

Explain whether you would use a survey, observational study, experiment, predictive model, or another approach, and why it fits your question.

Describe the population, who or what you would include, how you would select them, what you would measure, and how you would collect the information. Define the main concepts and explain how you would measure them.

Describe these plans without collecting data or conducting the study.

4. Sampling, Measurement, and Conclusions

Explain how your choices affect the evidence the study could produce.

  • Who would be represented, and who might be missed?
  • Could the sampling or measurement process bias the results?
  • What could the study tell the decision maker?
  • What generalizations or causal claims would the design support?
  • For a predictive study, how would you check performance on new cases?

5. Risks, Limitations, and Ethics

Discuss specific ways the proposed study could go wrong and how you would address them. Consider privacy, consent, fairness, and possible harm or exclusion. Explain what limitations would remain and how they would affect the conclusions.

6. Optional Supporting Detail

You may describe an expected pattern or briefly explain how you would analyze the proposed data. No calculations, statistical output, charts of results, or completed analysis are required. Keep any proposed analysis tied to the research question and study design.

Use the Research Proposal Outline to plan the paper and the Project 2 Rubric to check it.

Grading

The in-class work is worth 30 points. The written proposal is worth 70 points, scored across four criteria within the same document.

Criterion within the Written Research Proposal Points
Problem or opportunity 15
Research question and direction 15
Study design and measures 20
Sampling, measurement, conclusions, risks, and ethics 20
Written proposal total 70

Explain your design choices and their limitations clearly. A proposal that recognizes a problem and explains how to address it is more useful than one that overlooks it. Your grade reflects the proposed design and your reasoning, not results from research you have carried out.
