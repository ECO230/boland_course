## Lab 4: Written, Visual, and Oral Communication

### Purpose

Revise one quantitative message across 3 communication modes: a short written
paragraph, one presentation slide, and a brief spoken explanation for a
nontechnical business audience.

You must complete the associated lab preparation and participate in class to
earn credit for this lab.

### Learning objectives

By the end of the lab, you should be able to:

- write about data clearly and concisely;
- design a slide that makes one quantitative message easy to see; and
- explain evidence and implications without relying on technical jargon.

### In-class workflow

1. **Identify the message.** Underline the most important claim in your writing
   sample. Name the intended decision maker and the decision the evidence could
   inform.
2. **Revise the paragraph.** Lead with the takeaway, use everyday language,
   define the denominator and comparison, and remove details that do not help
   the audience understand the message.
3. **Design one slide.** Use a takeaway-first title and one relevant visual or
   small table. The slide should support what you say rather than duplicate a
   script.
4. **Deliver a 60-second explanation.** State the message, point to the most
   important evidence, explain why it matters, and end with an implication or
   next step.
5. **Exchange feedback.** Your partner should identify the main takeaway,
   strongest evidence, and one point that remained unclear.
6. **Revise once more.** Make one change to the paragraph, slide, or spoken
   explanation based on the feedback.

### Project connection

Apply the same approach to Project 1: establish one central message, curate the

### Completion check

Before leaving class, you should have a revised paragraph, one draft slide, and
brief feedback notes. Keep these materials for Homework 4 and later Project 1
work; there is no additional upload unless your instructor requests one.
